# OLT-022 — Owner-selected Genesis 1:1 «In / with» vertical under-word

## Binding presentation decision

Only for **Genesis 1:1**, print **In** as the primary English word, with **with directly underneath In** as a secondary linked under-word. Maintain OLT's usual conceptual colors (**In = purple #5B277D**, **with = blue #2463A6**) for a future styled ODT/PDF renderer. GitHub's Markdown preview uses a native table and subscript to ensure vertical placement; GitHub may not preserve custom text colors.

The English main line is unchanged:

> In the Beginning [of His Power] God — He shaped the Heavens and the Earth.

The original `[of His Power]` research footnote is preserved immediately after the brackets. The first Hebrew source word is the exact original OSHB token `01xeN`, `בְּ/רֵאשִׁ֖ית`, annotated as prepositional `בְּ` plus the `רֵאשִׁית` noun (original lemma `b/7225`, `HR/Ncfsa`). **With** is attached to the `בְּ` prefix, not misrepresented as a distinct original word or an alternate Hebrew lemma for `רֵאשִׁית`.

## Two readings and explicit evidence boundary

1. **Source-linked ordinary temporal reading:** *In the Beginning*. A standard Hebrew grammar explicitly uses Genesis 1:1 for temporal `בְּ`, contrasting with instrumental examples such as Genesis 3:19. [unfoldingWord Hebrew Grammar](https://uhg.readthedocs.io/en/stable/preposition.html).
2. **Owner's binitarian canonical reading:** *With the Beginning* — the owner identifies the Beginning as the **eternal, uncreated Son**, who worked with the Father in creation before incarnation. This is displayed as an **OWNER-SELECTED INTERPRETIVE UNDER-WORD**, not as a language-review-certified instrumental parsing of the prefix in this verse.
3. **Relevant independent scholarly discussion:** Brent A. Strawn (2022), *bĕ-rēʾšît, “With ‘Wisdom,’” in Genesis 1.1 (MT)*, *Journal for the Study of the Old Testament* 46(3), argues in the accessible journal abstract for a possible exegetical reading relating *Beginning* and *Wisdom* in Proverbs 8:22. This is useful evidence that an instrumental-like interpretive reading has been proposed, **not independent proof that “With the Son” is literally the Hebrew text**. [Abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03090892211032244).
4. **Contrasting linguistic discussion:** Benjamin Kantor (2025) argues that the temporal adverbial interpretation is compatible with ancient Hebrew reading traditions. The result does not exclude Christian theological typology but shows that **with** is not simply an established alternative reading agreed upon by scholars. [Journal abstract](https://doi.org/10.15699/jbl.1444.2025.2).

In OLT's source/dictionary fields, `with` therefore has category **BINITARIAN_CANONICAL_INTERPRETATION**, grammatical certainty `NOT_INDEPENDENTLY_ESTABLISHED` and separate evidence references. It is **not a verified lexical homonym**. The theological connection can mention John 1:1–3, 1 Corinthians 8:6 and Colossians 1:15–17 without inserting the Son as a new Hebrew source token.

## Scope guards

- Exactly **one exception: Genesis 1:1**, attached to source token `01xeN`; do not insert `with` under every other English *in* in Genesis or the Bible.
- The primary English sentence, original **seven Hebrew tokens**, Genesis 1's **434 source tokens**, owner's `[of His Power]` footnote and all owner-approved word families remain unchanged.

- The automatic contextual grammar engine proposed for other verses remains a separate, evidence-led future feature. This owner override is not a claim that such an automatic engine has been fully implemented.
