# OLT-009 — Hebrew source-first research for Genesis 1:2–5

The checkpoint produces 45 **individual real source-token alignments** and seven morphology-tagged wayyiqtol forms from the pinned OSHB/WLC data. All new English is research prose, not an approved chapter. It does not import an old Genesis draft.

## Four candidate verse lines
Genesis 1:2 — And the Earth — she was waste and emptiness; and darkness upon the face of the Deep; and the Spirit of God hovering upon the face of the Waters.

Genesis 1:3 — And God — He said: “Let light be.” And light was.

Genesis 1:4 — And God — He saw the light, that [he was] — good. And God — He separated between the light and between the darkness.

Genesis 1:5 — And God — He called to the light Day, and to the darkness He called Night; and was evening, and was morning: day one.

## Source-level boundaries
- Genesis 1:2 `הָיְתָה` is Qal qatal 3fs, `מְרַחֶפֶת` Piel active participle fem. sing. Neither is a wayyiqtol.
- Genesis 1:3 `יְהִי` is jussive, while `וַיְהִי` is wayyiqtol, rendered in English simple past.
- Genesis 1:4 the owner's **EXACT** approved opening clause is preserved. The bracketed `[he was]` is an English editorial supplement; the source `אֶת` object marker is counted with zero lexical gloss. The OSHB `light` noun source tag is common-gender and cannot alone establish masculine lexical gender; separate evidence informs this owner-approved stylistic choice.
- Genesis 1:5 `וַיִּקְרָא` is wayyiqtol while subsequent `קָרָא` is qatal. Both yield English `called`, documented with their distinct source codes.
- `רוּחַ` permits wind / breath / spirit; **Spirit of God** is a candidate context/theology choice. `תֹהוּ` and `בֹהוּ` remain two separate lexical senses.
- The light of Genesis 1 and John 1:4–9 can be compared as a **canonical typology proposal**, not as a Hebrew/Greek homonym or alternative OLT lexical translation. No colored under-word homonym claim is invented here.
- The general note about Hebrew grammatical gender stays **once on Bible page one**, not repeated at every verse.
