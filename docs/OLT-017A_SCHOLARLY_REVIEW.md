# OLT-017A — First scholarly review wave: contested Genesis 1 lexemes

**STATUS: RESEARCH, NOT AN INDEPENDENTLY CERTIFIED LEXICON OR APPROVED TRANSLATION.** This wave addresses five high-impact questions using visible external scholarship. Some findings rely solely on *publisher abstracts*, because full journal articles were not accessible. The source limitation is intentionally disclosed.

## 1. Genesis 1:1 — Bereshit syntax

Our primary research line is “In the Beginning”, with the owner's theological supplement “[of His Power]” now included in the MAIN English research line by OLT-018. The supplement remains separate from the seven Hebrew tokens and must have its explanatory footnote. Benjamin Kantor's 2025 *Journal of Biblical Literature* article argues, on the basis of historically attested Hebrew reading traditions, that the lack of an article can be compatible with an adverbial opening. Robert Holmstedt's 2022 abstract, however, argues that neither of two plausible syntactic analyses matches the traditional opening. This is a **genuine scholarly disagreement**. The English line remains a research candidate, not an adjudicated grammatical fact.

Sources: https://doi.org/10.15699/jbl.1444.2025.2 and https://journals.co.za/doi/10.25159/2663-6573/11647 . Both claims here are based on accessible abstracts.

## 2. Genesis 1:2 — Ruach Elohim

The term can be understood as wind, breath or spirit; “Spirit of God” remains the OLT research line, not an exclusive original lexical definition. Lemański's 2020 article and Reed Carlson's 2024 survey document the wider range of ruach. Theodore Hiebert's 2019 translation article advances a physical-material approach and objects to some traditional renderings; that argument is recorded as **a contested scholarly perspective**, not endorsed as consensus.

Sources: https://czasopisma.kul.pl/vv/article/view/1947 ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1476993X241234908 ; https://translation.bible/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/hiebert-2019-retranslating-genesis-1-2-reconnecting-biblical-thought-and-contemporary-experience.pdf .

## 3. Genesis 1:21 — Tanninim

“Great sea-creatures” is a contextual proposal rather than a claim about a modern zoological classification. The Hebrew can occur in serpentine, dragon-like and aquatic imagery elsewhere. Jacob F. Love's 2024 *Jewish Bible Quarterly* article discusses inconsistent English renderings across passages. Alternative dictionary possibilities are recorded for **future notes**, not stacked as invented slash-separated homonyms in the English line.

Sources: https://www.jewishbible.org/articles/presence-of-dragons-in-the-hebrew-bible/ and https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Gen&chapter=1&tab=commentaries&theme=false&verse=21 .

## 4. Genesis 1:26 — Tselem and demut

“Image” and “likeness” remain distinct source words. C. L. Crouch's 2010 study interprets them through a divine parentage framework, with Gen 5:1–3 as an analogue. Christian canonical comparisons to Christ as the image of God (Col 1:15; 2 Cor 4:4) are welcome as **exegetical crossreferences** but do not license translating the Hebrew word tselem as “Christ”.

Source: https://academic.oup.com/jts/article/61/1/1/1673983 . Research inference based on the article abstract.

## 5. Genesis 1:28 — Vekivshuha feminine suffix

The displayed original source morphology contains the Qal imperative 2mp **and** the 3fs suffix. Thus “and subdue her” is a grammatically transparent OLT English research proposal for the Earth. The degree and ethical force of the verb “subdue” still require contextual interpretation. This is not a second generic explanation about Hebrew grammatical gender: that convention belongs **once** at the opening of the Bible.

Morphology sources: https://lemma.gebible.app/v/genesis/1/28 and https://seethebible.org/bible/genesis/1/28 .

## Editorial outcome

No new English lexical family is approved. No homonym identity or Christian canonical parallel has been treated as a lexical equivalence. Existing owner locks bara -> shape, asah -> make, raqia -> firmament and conjunctional waw -> and stand. Any *future verified* homonym alternative is placed **under** the English word, never beside it with a slash.
